In a 730-square-kilometer island nation with zero tolerance for vehicular gridlock, Singapore's vehicle ownership market has long served as the ultimate laboratory for free-market rationing. Today, however, an analysis of 2,000 bidding rounds stretching from January 2010 through late November 2026 reveals that the system has crossed an irreversible psychological threshold.

A Certificate of Entitlement (COE) — which grants the bearer nothing more than the ten-year statutory right to put an automobile on the road, before paying a single cent for the car itself — has solidified at six figures. More astonishingly, Category A premiums for mainstream entry-level family cars (currently S$130,100, having breached an all-time peak of S$139,001 earlier this month) have systematically overtaken Category B luxury vehicles (S$125,600). This rare market inversion exposes the tectonic impact of Singapore's electric vehicle transition and commercial ride-hail fleet expansion.

The Anatomy of a Supply Squeeze: The Scissors Curve

At the root of Singapore's pricing explosion is the mechanistic ten-year statutory vehicle lifecycle. Because Singapore maintains an absolute zero vehicle population growth target, the number of fresh quota certificates issued in any quarter is fundamentally tied to the number of vehicles deregistered a decade prior.

Between 2018 and 2022, Singapore entered the cyclical nadir of this ten-year wave. Category A bi-monthly quotas dropped from over 1,800 units in 2018 to fewer than 500 units in late 2022 — an unprecedented 72% supply contraction. The economic response was brutally inelastic: as quota vanished, bids received held steadfast above 1.35 times quota, propelling Category A premiums from S$25,000 in 2018 to over S$100,000 by early 2023.

The 2023 "Cut-and-Fill" Policy Injection & The 2026 EV Inversion

By October 2023, with Category B hitting S$150,001 and Category E touching S$158,004, public outcry and corporate transport stress triggered unprecedented regulatory intervention. The Ministry of Transport (MOT) introduced the "Cut-and-Fill" mechanism: borrowing vehicle quota allocations projected from future peak deregistrations (2025–2027) and injecting them into the 2023–2024 supply pool.

While the injection successfully engineered a temporary price cooling in early 2024, the market was quickly overtaken by a second structural transformation: the rapid mass-market transition to Electric Vehicles (EVs). Automakers quickly optimized vehicle power outputs to slip below the 110kW threshold (such as the BYD Atto 3 100kW and Dolphin), channeling immense retail and private-hire vehicle (PHV) fleet demand directly into Category A.

Executive Summary
16-Year Supercycle Peak From a humble S$18,502 in early 2010 to S$139,001 in late 2026, Category A has appreciated by 651%, far outpacing CPI inflation and wage growth.
The 2026 Scissors Inversion Category A currently clears higher than Category B (S$130,100 vs S$125,600). Fleet operators and EV volume buyers have clustered in the lower category, exhausting supply.
Cut-and-Fill Limits Borrowing future supply cushioned 2024 prices, but did not resolve structural demand from ride-hail fleets and high-net-worth migration.

Strategic Decision Framework for Automotive Leaders

Actionable Directives

For Dealership Executives

De-tune powertrain offerings to 110kW: Align EV model configurations strictly with Cat A criteria, as consumer price elasticity collapses above the S$130,000 certificate threshold. Establish dynamic forward pricing contracts to protect floorplan inventory.

For Fleet & PHV Operators

Diversify into Cat C & Commercial EV platforms: Avoid bidding wars in Cat A by shifting urban delivery and logistics fleets into light commercial categories where quota volume is more predictable. Explore leasing models that amortize COE exposure.

For Transport Policymakers

Re-evaluate the 110kW EV threshold: The massive shift of high-torque electric sedans into Cat A has crowded out middle-class combustion buyers. A dedicated Clean Energy Vehicle (CEV) category would alleviate market distortion.
